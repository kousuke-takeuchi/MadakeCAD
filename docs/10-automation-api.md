# Automation & APIs

[日本語](10-automation-api.ja.md)

Everything the UI can do is scriptable. Three surfaces expose the same Command engine, so automated edits share undo history and update the UI live.

## Available

### MCP server (for AI clients)
Streamable HTTP at `127.0.0.1:9310/mcp` (`MADAKE_MCP_PORT` to change). Tools:
`open_project` / `save_project` (open or save a `.mdkproj`, with its chat history; opening replaces the drawing and is refused while an agent turn is running, and the in-app agent cannot open), `get_project`, `list_symbols` (bundled **and** user symbols; the bundled drawing aids are `connector_contact`, `power_rail`, `transistor_npn`, `transistor_pnp`, `photocoupler`, `opamp` and `tvs_diode`: a `power_rail` takes its net name from its **value** and joins same-named rails, labels and fixed-net marks, also across sheets, with an empty value warned as `erc.empty_net_name`; a `connector_contact` is a pass-through on a wire whose two pins are one node, its designator being the connector and its value the pole (the pole is also the pin key: the netlist, `get_pins`, `connect_pins` and the wire list write `J7:5` for the contact with value `5`, `J7.5#1` is its left pin and `J7.5#2` its right, and a blank value keeps the key `1`; the same pole of one connector on two different nets of the project netlist (nets joined across sheets by a label count as one net) is the error `erc.contact_pole_repeated`, reported once by the project check, on one net it is allowed), with the parts list counting one connector per designator and SPICE treating it as a zero-ohm bridge; the semiconductor parts have no simulation model; a symbol definition may carry `net_from_value` and `through` as well, in user symbol files too), `place_symbol` (returns the new part's pins; attribute `hide_reference: "true"` keeps the designator but does not print it), `draw_wire`, `get_pins` (pins of a placed part: number, name, paper position, direction), `connect_pins` (wire two pins by name, e.g. `U1.SDA` to `J1.2`, with a right-angle route; `connections` wires many in one undoable edit), `place_mate` (a plug/socket pair in one step; `mates` places many; an optional `pole` is stored on both halves and the From-To wire list then writes `REF:POLE` instead of `REF:1`, and a `mates` list that repeats a reference without poles numbers them 1, 2, 3 in list order), `connect_cable` (a multi-core cable drawn as one line on one end or both: `cores` names the cores, `a`/`b` give each end's `pins` or `part` and `style: multi` (one wire per core from the pins to a breakout) or `single` (the line lands on the part); instead of `pins`/`part`, a `multi` end may give `labels` (one net-label name per core, with `breakout_at` required): each core then gets a 10 mm wire running straight away from the run and ending in a net label of that name; breakout wires carry no gauge or colour of their own; cores map to pins in pin order; `cables` draws many in one undoable edit), `execute_commands` (full Command schema exposed), `get_netlist`, `run_verification`, `get_tidy_metrics` (the four counts plus `details`: each counted problem with its kind, entities, text and place, up to 50), `arrange_sheet` (align the parts of a sheet into columns and rows, open routing channels of `channel_mm` (default 8 mm), shrink gaps wider than `max_gap_mm` (default 20 mm), keep the order and redraw the wires of every cluster whose ends moved (clusters whose ends stay keep their wires); ground and power marks and labels that connect to one part only, and labels or marks placed directly on a pin, move with that part; `sheet_id` and optional `options` (`channel_mm`, `max_gap_mm`, `align_mm`, `route`; out of range is refused and nothing changes). A cluster that cannot be routed first keeps its old wire shape, stretched along the moved columns and rows with its ends on the pins (only when every pin, label, branch point and junction of the cluster stays on its wires, and it touches no other line, pin or label and crosses no other part's outline), and is listed in `kept_shape`; otherwise it is joined by net labels named after its own label, a label of the same net on another cluster, its net-name symbol or `ARR_<n>`, and listed in `labelled` (ground and power marks are never deleted). Cables, panel layouts, dimensions, port contacts, wires pointed at by other entities or FreeCAD, mixed length sources, clusters holding labels (or net-name symbols) with different names and results that do not fit the frame or run into the title block (with its revision rows) are refused, naming the nets where nets are the reason. One edit = one undo step. Reply: `moved`, `columns`, `rows`, `routed`, `labelled` (`[{net, label}]`), `kept_shape` (net names), `before`/`after` (the `get_tidy_metrics` numbers), `wire_length_mm` and `size_mm` (before and after)), `reroute_wires` (redraw the wires of a sheet automatically without changing any connection: right-angle wires that avoid symbols, text and other nets with few bends and crossings; `sheet_id`, `nets` (net names; default all) and optional `options` (`grid_mm` 0.5 to 10 mm, costs, clearance and margins finite and not negative; anything else is refused with an error and nothing changes); routing works on each physically connected wire group; groups that a cable run uses, whose wires differ in colour/gauge/part number/wire number or in where their lengths came from (`length_source`), that another entity (cable core, note leader, dimension) or a FreeCAD link points at, whose pin leads would touch another net, or that cannot be routed are left as they are and listed in `unchanged` with the reason; wire attributes carry over and the sum of the old `length_m` values goes on the longest new wire only; the automatic names of unnamed nets (N001 and so on) may change places after a reroute, because the new wires get new ids; one undoable edit, so one undo restores the original wires with their ids; the reply has `rerouted`, `unchanged` (`[{net, reason}]`), `before`/`after` tidy counts and `wire_length_mm` before/after), `draft_function_sheet` and `check_function_sheets` (draw a function sheet from a source project file and check the drafted sheets against it, see "Function sheet draft" below), `simulate_op`, `search_parts`, `upsert_part`, `delete_part`, `import_kicad`, `import_dxf` (DXF from AutoCAD Electrical / EPLAN; optional `wire_layers`), `list_terminal_blocks`, `get_terminal_chart`, `check_terminal_block`, `list_templates`, `apply_template`, `list_macros`, `save_macro`, `apply_macro`, `duplicate_symbol`, `save_user_symbol`, `delete_user_symbol`, the PLC I/O tools, `get_sheet_image` (the sheet as a PNG image, same look as the SVG export; `px_per_mm` and a `region` in mm to zoom in), `export_svg`, `export_pdf`, `export_dxf` (AutoCAD 2000 DXF), `export_kicad` (`.kicad_sch`), `export_report` (one of the seven reports as CSV or framed PDF), `export_pdf_book` (cover + all sheets + selected reports in one PDF), `export_bom`, `export_wire_list`, `undo`, `redo`.
Drawing edits that have no dedicated tool go through `execute_commands`: `set_revisions` (revision table), `set_sheet_annotation_text` (`height_mm` 1.0-3.5mm, or null for the 2.0mm default: the height of the wire notes, wire numbers and net tags on that sheet), `renumber_wires` (`mode: append | renumber`, optional `sheet_id`, `start`) and `set_wire_numbers` for wire numbers, `add_entity` with a `harness` entity for harness boundaries, `update_entity` writing `attrs["jumpers"] = "1-2,3-4"` on a terminal block symbol for saddle jumpers, `set_frame_template` for the frame template, `set_text_font` for the drawing font (`{"text_font": {"id": "kst32b", "latin": "half"}}` letters every text with the bundled stroke font, `latin: "full"` draws Latin letters full width, `null` returns to the system font; an unknown id is refused), `add_sheet` with `kind: "panel"` plus the `panel_outline` / `din_rail` / `duct` / `footprint` / `panel_legend` entities for panel layout, `renumber_panel_items` for balloon numbers, and `set_mech_link` / `remove_mech_link` / `set_wire_lengths` for the FreeCAD link (mechanical links and measured wire lengths).
Port pins: `place_symbol` with `attrs.block_pins` pins that carry `contacts` (`{"no":"U3","name":"USB-A メス","contacts":["VBUS","D-","D+","GND"]}`) makes one pin carry several conductors (2 to 32 contacts per pin, trimmed, unique within the pin); `get_pins` returns `contacts`; `get_netlist` splits such nets into `<name>.<contact>` with `contact` on the net and on port pins; `connect_cable` lands a side on one port pin with `pins: ["39.U3"]` and `style: single` (cores map to same-named contacts, or by order when no name matches and the counts are equal; a partial name match and `style: multi` on a port are refused).
Feed-through pins: one pin number may appear once on each of two opposite sides of a `block_pins` block (left and right, or top and bottom): the same pole with a wire on each side. Both sides need the same name (spaces trimmed) and neither may carry `contacts` or `connector`; the same number twice on one side, on neighbouring sides or three times is refused (`duplicate block pin number`). The two points are one electrical node and the net lists the pin once. `get_pins` returns both; a bare `U1.1` is ambiguous, so name `U1.1#1` or `U1.1#2`: they follow the block's pin table order (left, right, top, bottom as defined, before rotation or mirroring), not the sides as seen on screen, so on a mirrored block `U1.1#1` is drawn on the right. With `block_numbers: "inside"`, the number of a feed-through pair whose two pins lie on one line is drawn once, centred in the box, when the sides crossing that line (top and bottom for a left/right pair, left and right for a top/bottom pair) have no pins; otherwise each pin draws its own number. File format 23.
Connector pictures: a `block_pins` pin may also carry `connector` (one of `usb_a_f usb_a_m usb_c_f usb_c_m micro_b_f micro_b_m jack35_f jack35_m dc_f dc_m screw jst_ph_f jst_xh_f`; `_f` socket, `_m` plug) and, for `screw` / `jst_ph_f` / `jst_xh_f`, `connector_ref` (consecutive pins with the same ref form one body, labelled `J3 PH` / `TB1`). An unknown kind, a blank ref, a ref on another kind and one ref used by two groups are refused. A connector pin's point is 5 mm from the box, so read positions with `get_pins`; `get_pins` and the netlist do not report the kind.
Fine pitch: `place_symbol` with `attrs.block_pitch: "2.5"` (default `"5"`) gives a block a 2.5 mm pin pitch and a 2.5 mm size step, with a 5 x 5 mm minimum; a side with n pins needs at least (n + 1) x 2.5 mm. The outline and the pin rows are shifted by 1.25 mm where needed so that pins and edges stay on the 2.5 mm grid, so read positions with `get_pins`. `attrs.block_numbers: "inside"` (default `"outside"`) draws the pin numbers inside the outline and does not draw the pin names, so show signal names with net labels on the wires. Other values are refused, and a connector picture (`connector`) cannot be used on a 2.5 mm block or on a block with numbers inside.
Unnumbered pins, spacer rows, captions and text size: in `block_pins`, a pin may leave out `no` (or give an empty one) when its `name` is unique within the block; nothing is printed outside the box for it, and its name is the pin key everywhere (`get_pins` returns it as `number`, `connect_pins` takes `U1.EN`, and the netlist and the KiCad / DXF export use it). With the numbers inside, such a pin shows its name where the number would be. Two pins without a number that share a name, or a name equal to another pin's number, are refused, and an unnumbered pin cannot be a feed-through pin. Each side array may also hold `{"gap": n}` (n = 1 to 20; leaves n pin positions empty on that side) and `{"caption": "text"}` (takes one row and prints the text inside the box at that row, at the pin-name size, in bold, left aligned on the left side and right aligned on the right side; on the top and bottom sides it sits on that column). Neither is a pin, neither has a connection point, and `get_pins` skips both; a block may use at most 400 rows in all, counting pins, captions and gaps. `attrs.block_pin_text` (1.5 to 5.0 mm, default 2) sets the height of pin names, captions and pin numbers, and the minimum width of the box grows so that names do not meet the opposite side. `attrs.block_number_align` = `"edge"` ends each outside number on the left and right sides 0.5 mm outside the box edge, so the text runs away from the box along the lead and never into the box or a pin name; without it (or with any other value) numbers stay centred on the lead exactly as before. Pin positions do not change. Function sheet drafts set it on every box. Pin positions stay on the grid of the block's pitch, so read them with `get_pins`. The file format version is 29: older builds refuse these files by version number rather than draw a block without its pins.

Views (one unit drawn on several sheets): a block with the attribute `block_view` = `"1"` is a view (`block::is_view`); set it by hand with `update_entity` or `place_symbol` attrs. Views that share a reference are one part for checking and listing: `erc.duplicate_reference` does not report them (on one sheet or across sheets), while a view together with an ordinary symbol of the same reference is still reported; the parts list counts them once (quantity 1, the reference listed once) and takes the part number from the first view, in sheet order and then drawing order, whose value is not blank; the cross-sheet location text (`/2.B3`) is not printed beside a view; and the project netlist lists each pin under the shared reference, so the netlist of all views equals the netlist of the whole unit. `run_verification` over the project adds `erc.view_pin_repeated` (error, both views named) when two views of one reference show the same pin key (the number, or the name for an unnumbered pin), and `erc.view_part_mismatch` (warning) when two views carry different non-blank part numbers. A view without a reference is an ordinary block. The attribute is a string in `attrs` and is kept by the KiCad and DXF export and import; the file format version is unchanged (29).

Drawing appearance: `set_sheet_wire_style` (`sheet_id`, `weight_mm` 0.18-1.0 mm or null for the 0.35 mm default, `hide_notes` true to stop drawing the wire notes — gauge, colour word, length — while the wires keep their colour) through `execute_commands`. All values of this command are always set together: a value that is left out goes back to its default (for example, leaving out `hide_notes` shows the notes again). The junction dot grows with a thick wire (radius 0.9 times the weight, never below 0.6 mm), and the fan-out lines of a cable follow the weight. `place_symbol` with `attrs.block_caption: "inside"` (default `"above"`) writes the reference and the value in the middle of the block outline instead of above it; nothing moves out of its way, so use it on boxes whose middle row is free (a pin name or an inside number on the middle row is overwritten). A note (`text` entity) with `rotation` 90, 180 or 270 is drawn turned clockwise about its anchor point; its selection box and its leader follow the turned text.

Tags that say where the wire continues: a net label with `attrs.tag` (`left` / `right`) is drawn as a flag. `set_sheet_xref_style` (`sheet_id`, `style`) through `execute_commands` chooses how the destination is written: `zone` (default) puts the zone address (`/2.B3`) in small text outside the flag; `sheet_name` writes the names of the other sheets that carry a label of the same name inside the flag, after the name (in sheet order, joined with a middle dot; three or more sheets give the first two plus an "other" mark; the name is bold, the destination is 0.8 of its size, and the flag grows to hold both). In `sheet_name` style nothing is drawn outside the flag, and a flag whose name is on no other sheet shows the name only. A tagged label may also carry `attrs.tag_dest` (free text that replaces the automatic destination in both styles, for a destination that is not a sheet of the project; blank means automatic) and `attrs.tag_flow: "out"` (the pointed end moves to the far end of the body so that the flag points away from the wire; any other value keeps the normal shape). Untagged labels keep the zone address outside in both styles. Net names that contain a dot (for example `J7.2`) are ordinary names unless the part before the dot is a label on a port net that has a contact of that name; in that case the wire taps that contact.

Line and lettering style: `set_sheet_wire_style` also takes `symbol_weight_mm` (outline weight of symbols and blocks, 0.18-1.0 mm, null for the 0.3 mm default) `vivid_colors` (true draws wires in pure colours; the colour names do not change) and `hide_numbers` (true stops drawing the wire numbers, that is the net names and numbers written on wires; the names stay in the data); all five values are set together. A `wire` entity may carry `dashed: true` and its own `weight_mm` (0.18-2.0 mm). A `shape` line or polyline may carry `arrow_end: true`. `set_text_font` takes `pen_mm` (0.1-1.0 mm): every text is then stroked at that width whatever its size; `spacing` (0.3-3): a letter-spacing factor for every text of the drawing; and `stretch` (0.3-3): a horizontal scale of the glyphs and their advance, for drawings lettered with narrow characters (it does not apply to frame text, which keeps the widths of its template cells). A `text` entity may carry its own `stretch` too (it multiplies the font's). A `text` entity may carry its own `spacing`, which multiplies the font's (frame template cells multiply it too). `place_symbol` with `attrs.block_leads: "none"` (only with `block_numbers: "inside"`) draws no leads and puts the pins on the block outline, so wires run straight to the box. Any symbol may carry `attrs.caption_layout`, a JSON object `{"ref": {"x","y","h","anchor","hide"}, "value": {...}}` that places the reference and the value line by line (offsets in mm from the placement point, `y` is the baseline, `anchor` start/middle/end, `hide` true to drop the line); a line left out stays where it was.
Claude Code connects automatically via this repository's `.mcp.json`.

### Link API (REST + SSE, for external tools)
`127.0.0.1:9310/api/v1` — plain JSON REST used by the CLI, browser-based UI verification, and (future) the FreeCAD add-on:
- Read: `/project`, `/symbols` (+`/symbols/user`), `/frame-templates`, `/stroke-fonts/{id}` (the glyphs of a bundled stroke font, e.g. `kst32b`; 404 for an unknown id), `/panel/unplaced`, `/netlist`, `/verify`, `/search`, `/devices`, `/parts`, `/wire-parts`, `/terminals`, `/terminals/chart`, `/terminals/check`, `/plc/modules`
- Write: `/commands` (Command array; `?batch=true` runs the whole array as **one** edit = one undo step, and applies nothing at all if a command is refused), `/undo`, `/redo`, `/save`, `/load`, `/import/kicad`, `/import/dxf`, `/simulate/op` (body: `case_id` = a saved study case, `open_switches`, `overrides` = reference → {attribute: value} for one run, `shorts` = net names shorted to ground; the result adds `findings`, rating violations most severe first, and `loads`, current / voltages / limit / limit kind / ratio per wire and part), `/route/reroute` (same body and reply as `reroute_wires`), `/route/arrange` (same body and reply as `arrange_sheet`), `/symbols/user/{save,delete}`, `/symbols/duplicate`, `/macros/{build,save,apply,apply-inline}`, `/templates/apply`, `/variants/{start,finish}`, `/export/{svg,pdf,dxf,kicad,pdf-book,report,bom,wire-list}`, parts CRUD
- Live updates: `GET /events` (SSE patch stream)
- Local-origin guard: requests from non-local web origins are rejected

### madake CLI
Thin terminal client over the Link API: `status`, `project`, `netlist`, `verify`, `sim`, `reroute` (`--sheet <ID>`, repeatable `--net <NAME>`; `--json` prints the raw reply), `arrange` (`--sheet <ID>`, `--channel <MM>`, which also raises `max_gap_mm` to the channel when it is above 20 mm; `--json` prints the raw reply), `draft <request.json>` (function sheet draft, see below; `--json` prints the raw reply), `draft-check <source.mdkproj>` (repeatable `--ignore <REF>`; exits non-zero when the sheets do not match the source or a tag flag leaves the frame), `parts`, `terminals` (terminal block list), `export`, `save`/`open` (incl. `.kicad_sch` and `.dxf`, the latter with `--wire-layer`), `renumber` (wire numbering: `--sheet`, `--mode append|renumber`, `--start`), `exec` (Command array from JSON), `undo`/`redo`. `--json` for machine-readable output.

`madake export <kind> <path>` covers both schematics and reports:

| Kind | Options |
|---|---|
| `svg`, `pdf`, `dxf`, `kicad` | `--sheet <ID>` (default: first sheet). `dxf` = AutoCAD 2000 DXF for ACADE/EPLAN, `kicad` = `.kicad_sch` |
| `pdf-book` | `--reports wire-list,terminal-chart,terminal-diagram,bom,xref,panel-placement` (in that order), `--no-cover` |
| `bom`, `wire-list`, `terminal-chart`, `terminal-diagram`, `xref-table`, `plc-io`, `panel-placement` | `--format csv\|pdf` (default: from the file extension — `.pdf` writes framed drawing sheets), `--terminal <ref\|id>` for the two terminal reports |

Reports always cover the whole project; the only narrowing is `--terminal TB1` (add `--sheet <ID>` when the same designator exists on several sheets). `madake terminals` lists the candidates.

## Function sheet draft

A large wiring drawing reads well on paper when it is divided into sheets with one function per sheet. The draft command makes one such sheet in the open project from a **source** project file that holds the complete wiring. The caller decides **what** goes on the sheet (which units, in which column from left to right); MadakeCAD decides **how** it is drawn (box contents, pin sides and order, wires, tags, power and ground marks, spacing). The source file is only read, never written, and it is never the open project.

Entry points: MCP tools `draft_function_sheet` and `check_function_sheets`, Link API `POST /api/v1/draft/function-sheet` and `POST /api/v1/draft/check`, CLI `madake draft <request.json>` and `madake draft-check <source.mdkproj> [--ignore REF]...`. A draft is one edit: one undo removes the whole sheet.

Request (the Link API body and the MCP input; the CLI reads the same object from a JSON file and resolves a relative `source_path` against the file's folder):

```json
{
  "source_path": "/path/to/source.mdkproj",
  "name": "2 Control",
  "columns": [["U1", "U2"], ["TB1"]],
  "pins": {"U1": ["1", "2", "5"]},
  "power_nets": ["P24X"],
  "ground_nets": ["GND"],
  "destinations": {"J7.2": "cabinet B"},
  "paper": "A3",
  "pitch_mm": 5.0,
  "planned_sheets": ["1 Power", {"name": "2 Control", "units": ["U2"], "pins": {"U2": ["3", "4"]}}, "3 Motors"]
}
```

- `columns` (required): one to eight columns, each a list of unit references (all symbols of the source that share one reference), top to bottom; a reference may appear once.
- `pins`: limits a unit to the listed pin keys, so one unit can be divided over several sheets. Without an entry the view carries every pin of the unit that is in use (its source net has two or more pins).
- `power_nets`, `ground_nets`: source net names. Their pins get no wire: pins of one unit on one side that share the net sit in adjacent rows and are joined at their stub ends by one vertical bar, and the group gets one mark, led out from the bar (power from the upper end one pitch up, ground from the lower end one pitch down, then away from the box) so that its text is clear of the bar, the stubs and the pin numbers; `marked` counts marks, not pins. `ground_nets` defaults to every source net that carries a net mark symbol.
- `destinations`: net name to free text for a net that continues outside the project (it becomes the tag's destination text).
- `planned_sheets` (optional): pass the names of all sheets you will draft so that tags keep room for them. Each entry is a name string, or an object `{"name", "units", "pins"?}` naming the units (and optionally the pin keys, as the request's `pins`) that planned sheet carries; the two forms may be mixed, and the list may be in any order and may include this request's own name and names that already exist. A blank or repeated name, a unit the source does not have, or a pin key the unit does not have is refused. A tag then reserves exactly the room its own flag can need: the other sheets that show a label of its net name (existing sheets, and planned objects that carry a pin of the net) and every name-only entry (content unknown, so it counts for every tag); one name gives that name, two or more give the two widest joined as the flag joins them (plus the other mark for three or more), none gives no room at all. Without `planned_sheets` the old room is kept. If a sheet that carries the same net is drafted later without being planned, the flag of an earlier sheet can grow past its room and out of the frame; `draft-check` reports such tags in `tags_outside`.
- A block unit becomes a block with `block_view` = `1` (one unit on several sheets is one part for the duplicate-reference check, the parts list and the netlist). A library symbol (a resistor, a relay coil) is placed as it is and may appear on one function sheet only.
- Stub length is per unit side: max(5 mm, width of the widest printed pin number on that side + 2.5 mm) rounded up to the 2.5 mm grid. Carried pins of one unit and one side on the same net are one group (the bar, with a junction wherever three ends meet); a net's group is one wire terminal (the bar end nearest its peers, the middle when peers are above and below), and a net whose pins are all in one group (a jumper inside a unit) is only the bar. A net whose pins are all on the sheet is wired (orthogonal, off symbols and text). A net with pins on other sheets gets its carried pins wired and one tag, at the pin that comes first in column order: with one carried pin the tag sits at the end of that pin's stub (bar); with more, it hangs 5 mm beyond the stub on the same row, flag pointing away from the box, and the wire to the other pins leaves before the flag, so no wire runs through or along a flag. Tags and marks (with their text) are obstacles for the wires, and a tag reserves room for the other sheets' names that appear inside it later (the wider of its destination, the longest sheet name of the open project and 10 characters). Symbols, pins, stub ends, tags and marks are on the 2.5 mm grid; bends of routed wires may lie on the 1.25 mm router grid; wires stay inside the frame and out of the title block. The tag name is the net's label, or `<reference>.<pin>` of the first pin of the whole source net, so every sheet computes the same name. The new sheet shows the names of the other sheets inside the tags (`xref_style` = `sheet_name`). Columns are packed from the left edge of the drawing area: column k+1 starts at the right extent of column k (boxes, stubs, bars, tags, marks) plus a channel of max(20 mm, 10 mm + 2.5 mm per net crossing it) on the 2.5 mm grid; a tag reserves the names of the sheets of the open project that already carry its net (two names and the other mark, at least the longest sheet name or 10 characters); a request whose columns would cross the drawing area is refused with the column and the excess in mm. A pin whose peers on the sheet are all in its own column goes on the right side (on the left in the last column), and same-column peers count as right (left in the last column) in mixed cases; such nets count for the channel to the right of the column. Every box ends its outside numbers at the box edge (`block_number_align` = `edge`). No wire crosses or touches a tag flag: flags (with their reserved room) are hard obstacles, and a net that cannot be routed without touching one is joined instead by tags without destination at the upper ends of its bars (stub ends), which are obstacles for the other nets too; it is listed in `labelled`.

Reply: `sheet_id`, `units`, `pins`, `wired` (net names), `tagged` (`[{net, pins}]`), `marked` (`{power, ground}`), `labelled`, `skipped_pins` (`[{reference, pin, reason}]`) and `metrics` (the `get_tidy_metrics` numbers of the new sheet).

Refused with a message and no change: source file missing or unreadable; a blank reference; a reference the source does not have or listed twice; a library-symbol unit drawn by several symbols in the source (a relay coil with its contacts); a library-symbol unit already on a sheet of the open project; a pin key the unit does not have; a net in `power_nets` / `ground_nets` / `destinations` the source does not have, or in both power and ground; a sheet name already in use; a pin already shown by a view on another sheet; no carried pin; a net through a cable core or a port with contacts; columns that do not fit the paper (the message names the column and the excess in mm); the open project changing again and again while the plan is made (three tries).

Check: `check_function_sheets { source_path, ignore_references? }` compares the source with the open project and changes nothing. Both sides are reduced to sets of `(reference, pin)` per net (nets with fewer than two pins, net mark symbols and pass-through symbols are left out; net names are not compared), and `ignore_references` are left out on both sides.

```json
{
  "ok": false,
  "source_nets": 12,
  "matched": 10,
  "missing_pins": [{"reference": "U3", "pin": "4", "source_net": "P24X"}],
  "extra_pins": [],
  "split": [{"source_net": "SIG_A", "parts": [["U1:3"], ["U2:3", "U4:1"]]}],
  "merged": [],
  "tags_outside": [],
  "layout_ok": true
}
```

`missing_pins` are in the source and on no sheet; `extra_pins` are on a sheet and not in the source; `split` lists pins of one source net that are not one net in the open project (a tag name that differs between sheets shows up here); `merged` lists pins of different source nets that are one net in the open project. `tags_outside` (`[{sheet, net, excess_mm}]`, sorted by sheet order, then net) lists every tag whose flag, drawn with the destination text the SVG export prints, reaches beyond the inner frame area of its sheet or into the title block; `excess_mm` is the largest overhang with one decimal. `layout_ok` is true when that list is empty; `ok` stays "connections equal" and is not affected. `madake draft-check` prints the same and exits with a non-zero status when `ok` or `layout_ok` is false (`--json` prints the raw reply).
