# Schematic Editor

[日本語](03-schematic-editor.ja.md)

A purpose-built editor for industrial electrical schematics, modeled on the conventions of AutoCAD Electrical-class tools: ribbon UI, a dark drafting canvas with millimeter coordinates, and strict grid discipline (2.5 mm pin pitch).

## Available

- **Canvas**: pan (middle button / Space), wheel zoom, grid display, paper outline, crosshair cursor. Coordinates in mm, top-left origin
- **Symbols**: 51 bundled JIS C 0617 / IEC 60617 symbols — supplies and conversion (AC source, transformer, bridge rectifier, protective/frame earth), protection (fuse, 1/2/3-pole circuit breakers, disconnectors), operating switches (pushbuttons NO/NC, emergency stop, changeover and three-position switches, limit switches), relays and contactors (coil, NO/NC/changeover contacts, 3-pole contactor), semiconductors, passive parts, loads (motors 3-phase/single-phase/DC, lamp, bell, and the pictorial **loudspeaker**, **buzzer**, **siren** and **fan** — established JIS C 0617 / IEC 60617 diagram symbols only, each cited to its standard item — loudspeaker and fan to their IEC 60617 item numbers, buzzer and siren to JIS C 0617-8:2011 §10 (their IEC identity numbers are unconfirmed) — no invented artwork), instruments (voltmeter, ammeter, current transformer) and connectors — plus **parametric symbols** `connector_{n}p` and `terminal_block_{n}p` with 1–50 poles at 5 mm pitch, and the **generic block** (`block`) described below. A `terminal_block_{n}p_{levels}l` variant (1–3 levels, e.g. `terminal_block_8p_2l`) draws one terminal column per level side by side (poles as rows, so `1a` and `1b` share a row), numbered with a level letter (`1a`, `1b`, …); one level never short-circuits with another, every connection point stays on the 2.5 mm grid with room for a wire between the columns, and the whole thing is still one device in the terminal chart, connection diagram and BOM. Every symbol carries pin connection directions, attribute slots (reference, part number, description, rating) and bilingual search keywords; multi-pole devices are numbered pole by pole (1/2, 3/4, 5/6) and conduct pole by pole. Terminal blocks model feed-through terminals (left/right points of a terminal are internally connected); a terminal block's jumpers (`attrs["jumpers"]`) are drawn directly on the schematic symbol as a thick shorting bar filling the gap between the two jumpered terminals' marks, in addition to the terminal chart and connection diagram. On a multi-level block a jumper is written with the level letters (`1a-1b` across the levels of one pole, `1a-2a` along one level); a plain numeric spec there is refused instead of being guessed at
- **Pin-group brackets**: on a `block`, `terminal_block_*` or `connector_*` instance, `attrs["pin_groups"]` (`"1-4:power,5-8:signal"`, comma-separated `range:caption` items) draws a square-bracket beside the pin column from the first pin of the range to the last, with the caption beside it split into 4-character lines to approximate vertical writing. The range names pins in **chart order** (the order the symbol declares them — a multi-level terminal block's `1a-4a` stays within one level), and follows the pin's own direction, so it tracks any rotation or mirror automatically. The bracket sits beyond the pin stubs and the caption beyond the bracket, and the designator, type and function labels are placed clear of both; on a multi-level terminal block the bracket goes against the outer edge of that level's own column. An overlapping range, an unknown pin or an unparseable item is refused as an ERC diagnostic naming the device and the bad item, and only that item is left undrawn — the rest of the drawing is unaffected. **A pin whose own number contains a hyphen cannot be named in a group** (block pin numbers are free text, and `-` is the range separator): such an item is refused with a diagnostic saying exactly that. **A range that spans two levels of a multi-level terminal block** (e.g. `4a-1b`) cannot be bracketed either — each level is bracketed from its own outer side, so one bar cannot hold both — and is refused with the same diagnostic rather than quietly drawing nothing. Pin groups on a coil drawn as a device block are reported the same way, because a device block has no pin column to bracket
- **Placement**: insert dialog with search, per-category grid, pole-count stepper for parametric parts, and a parts-database section that applies part number and rated current on placement. Ghost preview, `R` to rotate, automatic reference designators (F1, K2, TB1, …)
- **Device blocks with named pins**: a controller, a drive or an amplifier is a box whose pins carry names rather than ordinals. Insert *Block (generic)* and fill in the **pin table** — one row per pin, per edge, with a pin number and a pin name — and the symbol is generated from it: names inside the outline, numbers outside on the lead, pins on the 2.5 mm grid at 5 mm pitch, outline size automatic or given. The shape lives on the instance (`attrs.block_pins`, `attrs.block_size`), so no library entry is needed and every block on the sheet can differ. Netlist, ERC, BOM, terminal chart and both renderers use the generated definition, and a block with a duplicate or empty pin number is refused at command time. The same pin table is reachable from the properties panel (*Edit pin table…*), and confirming it is one undo step. A pin can also say which **physical connector** it is (*Connector* column: USB-A, USB-C or micro-B socket or plug, 3.5 mm jack or plug, DC jack or plug, screw terminals, JST PH or XH socket): a small black-and-white picture is drawn on the box edge — a socket as an open body set into the edge, a plug as a black insert sticking out — and that pin's connection point moves 5 mm out from the box (ERC then reports the wire end to tidy up, or the pin if the wire no longer reaches it). Consecutive screw-terminal or JST pins with the same *Group name* (`J3`, `TB1`) share one body labelled `J3 PH` / `TB1`. The picture is drawing-only: contacts, nets and reports do not change. It appears on the canvas and in SVG, PDF, PNG, DXF and KiCad output (DXF and KiCad write one block definition per distinct block shape, and read it back as a block with its pin table)
- **Wiring**: orthogonal polyline tool with grid snap and pin snap (diamond marker); junctions; net labels; free text annotations
- **Wire annotations**: every wire prints its own `0.75sq white 0.4m` note automatically on its longest segment, opposite the wire number, from the gauge, colour and cut length stored on the wire — see [Wire management](05-wire-management.md)
- **Directional net-label tags**: a net label can be drawn as a five-cornered flag pointing left or right (properties panel → *Tag shape*), so the reader sees at a glance whether the signal leaves or arrives there. The frame follows the text width; labels without a tag are drawn exactly as before
- **Annotation styling**: a free note carries an anchor (left / centre / right), a **colour token** (`default`, `accent`, `warning`, `success`, `muted`) and a bold flag. Colours are semantic, not literal — screen and paper each pick a legible colour for the token — and raw colour codes are refused. Every symbol also has a third text field, the **function label** (e.g. "emergency stop"), printed by the symbol's `FUNCTION` slot or, if it has none, under the outline
- **Note leader lines**: a free note can carry a leader (`leader_to`) pointing at any other entity on the same sheet. A straight line is drawn from the midpoint of whichever edge of the note's own bounding box sits closest to the target, to the nearest point on the target's bounding box, with a filled dot (the same size as a wire junction) at the target end — recomputed every time either the note or the target moves, so it always follows. A `leader_to` naming an entity that does not exist on the sheet, or the note itself, is refused; if the target is deleted later the note simply draws no leader and verification reports the stale target. There is no properties-panel control for it yet (the panel has no entity-pick widget to build on); set it through a command (`UpdateEntity` on the note), the CLI's `exec`, or an MCP tool
- **Editing**: click/shift/marquee selection, drag-move (grid-snapped), delete, full undo/redo (⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z) — every edit from every entry point (UI, AI, CLI, API) shares one history
- **Sheets**: multiple sheets per project with tab switching and adding. A sheet's paper size and orientation can be changed after creation (**Paper size** dialog, Schematic tab → Edit group, or the `set_sheet_paper` command) without moving or losing anything already drawn on it — entities left outside the new paper are reported as an ERC warning instead
- **Project information**: project name and number, plus the company/drawing-number/title fields of every sheet's title block, set together in one command (**Project information** dialog, Schematic tab → Edit group, or `set_project_meta`) — see [Standards output](04-standards-output.md) for how they feed the title-block binds
- **Mating connector pairs**: a connector instance (`connector_*` family) can declare its mate by reference designator (properties panel → *Mate*, or `attrs["mates_with"]`); the pair is checked by ERC and listed in the wire list and from-to report, with a short dashed line drawn on the sheet when both are close together — see [Wire management](05-wire-management.md)
- **Wire numbers**: net-scoped automatic numbering (top-up / renumber, start number, one sheet or the whole project), per-net editing in the properties panel, and a display class of their own
- **Harness boundaries**: named dashed enclosures for a group of wires made and bought as one bundle; every wire fully inside one is reported in the harness column of the wire list. An enclosure or case outline is drawn with a *graphics* rectangle instead, so it leaves the reports alone
- **Graphics**: rectangles, lines, circles and polylines drawn as pure annotation — a colour token, a stroke width of 0.1-2.0 mm, an optional light fill and a dashed option. Drawn behind the wires. Graphics carry no electrical meaning, so they never appear in the netlist, the checks, the parts list or the wire list
- **Wire part-number legend block**: an auto-drawn table (Schematic tab → Wiring group → *Wire legend*, click to place) listing colour / gauge / part number / total length for the wires on that sheet — one row per distinct combination, sorted by colour, then gauge, then part number, with the summed length in the last column. Colours are grouped under the name the wire annotation prints, so wires recorded as `red` and as `赤` share one row. It holds no content of its own: every row is derived from the sheet's wires each time it is drawn, resolving the part number the same way the wire list does (the wire's own number first, then the project's wire-part table). A sheet with no wires still draws the header row. The table is a **fixed 90 mm wide** (columns of 24 / 14 / 30 / 22 mm) and 5 mm per row, so on a dense A4 sheet it may have nowhere to go — place it on a large sheet, or in the margin a corner of the drawing leaves free. Fixed 2.0 mm text, black rule lines, no fill. Placed/moved/deleted like any other entity; it carries no electrical meaning, so like graphics it never appears in the netlist, the checks or the wire list — see [Wire management](05-wire-management.md)
- **Circuit macros**: save the current selection as a reusable macro (name, category, base point = the bottom-left pin of the selection, variant `A`) into `~/MadakeCAD/macros`, then insert it from the **Macros** tab of the insert dialog. Placement shows a ghost, `R` rotates, `Tab` switches variants. On insert, reference designators are renumbered above the highest already used in the project and wire numbers are cleared, so a macro can be dropped twice on the same sheet without collisions. `⌘C` / `⌘V` copy and paste a selection through the same mechanism as an unnamed macro. One insert is one edit — a single `⌘Z` takes the whole circuit back
- **Macro variants from the save dialog**: typing in the name field lists existing macros whose name contains the text; picking one saves the selection as a **variant of that macro** (A–Z, up to 26, as in EPLAN) instead of a new macro. The chips show the registered keys (✓) and the next free key, which is selected by default; the save button reads *Save as variant C*, and two previews compare the saved variant A with this selection. Choosing a registered key asks before overwriting it, with *Save to another variant* as the way out. Name, category, placeholders and value sets stay those of the macro; a placeholder that pointed at a symbol in another variant also binds to the symbol with the **same designator** in the new variant, so a value set fills it in whichever variant is inserted. Saving is a file operation (not on the undo stack). MCP `save_macro` takes the same `id` + `variant`
- **Macro value sets**: a macro can declare **placeholders** — named slots such as "motor rating" or "fuse part number", each pointing at the value or an attribute (`attrs.<name>`) of specific entities in the macro — and any number of **value sets** that fill every placeholder at once. The save dialog lists the placeholders and the value sets; the insert dialog shows a value-set dropdown for macros that have one. Choosing "400 V / 5.5 kW" before dropping the circuit writes the ratings and part numbers into every symbol it covers, still as **one undo step**. An unnamed `⌘C` / `⌘V` macro has no value sets, and older macro files without the fields keep loading unchanged. A value set that names an unknown placeholder or a missing entity is refused without altering the drawing
- **Coil ⇔ contact cross-references**: symbols that share a reference designator (a `K1` coil and its contacts) are one device. A contact map is drawn under the coil — IEC 60947-1 terminal pairs (`13-14`, `23-24`, …) against each contact's `/sheet.zone` address — and every contact carries the coil's address beside it, printed to whichever side (right, then above, then below the outline) does not collide with a wire annotation already there. When the part's contact configuration is known (`contact_config`, e.g. `2NO+2NC`), unused contacts are listed as `—`, and using more contacts than the device has is an ERC error
- **Physical device block (relay coils)**: a coil is drawn distributed (the small coil symbol plus the contact map underneath, above) by default; ticking *Show as physical block* in the properties panel (`attrs.device_block`) draws it instead as one outline carrying the designator, the part number and a contact image — one line per contact sharing the designator, with that contact's IEC terminal pair and `/sheet.zone` address. The contacts stay individually placed and wired either way, and the coil's own pins do not move, so toggling the checkbox never breaks a wire — the table itself is always drawn upright, and rotating or mirroring the coil moves only its pins, whose lead lines then run from each pin along that pin's own axis — the direction its wire comes in on — to the block's outline. Click anywhere inside the block to select the coil; the contact image belongs to the *reference designators* view class, so hiding that class leaves the outline and its header. SVG/PDF export follows the same outline
- **Device-level cross-sheet reference**: any designator whose instances span more than one sheet gets an automatic `/2.B3`-style note beside every instance, naming the other sheets it appears on (several sheets: `/2.B3 /3.C1`, same one-space join as the net-label cross-reference described in [Standards output](04-standards-output.md)). This generalises the net-label reference to any device. Only the coils and contacts that the coil ⇔ contact reference above already writes an address for are skipped here, so a relay coil with no contacts anywhere — which that reference leaves unannotated — still gets the ordinary device reference
- **Project-wide search (⌘F)**: a floating search bar over the canvas with filter chips (designator, part no., net, text) and a docked result panel (designator / kind / location / note). `Enter` and `Shift+Enter` walk the hits, `Esc` closes; picking a hit switches sheet, selects the entity and zooms to it
- **Device navigator**: the third tab of the left panel lists every device as a reference-designator tree expanded to its functions (coil, contacts with their terminal pairs, terminals, body) with each function's `/sheet.zone`. Click a row to jump to it; right-click to jump or delete (deletion goes through the command engine, so `⌘Z` restores it)
- **Reserved contacts (unplaced functions)**: a relay whose coil carries a contact configuration (`contact_config`, e.g. `2NO+1NC`, from the parts database) lists the contacts that are not drawn yet as `◌ contact 23-24 NO … unplaced` rows, numbered like the unused rows of its contact map. Click a row (or *Place* in its context menu) to put that contact on the cursor — a chip next to the cursor reads *Place K1 contact 23-24 NO* — and click to drop it, or drag the row straight onto the drawing. The contact keeps the designator (`K1`, not renumbered), the tool returns to Select after one contact, and the placement is one ordinary `add_entity`, so `⌘Z` takes it back. Contacts outside the configuration are never offered (drawing extra ones by hand still raises the contact-overuse check). Filter chips above the tree show *All*, *Unplaced* (devices with something left to place) or *Placed*. *Place all unplaced…* is not implemented yet and only logs so
- **Reference surfer**: Alt(Option)-click a symbol with a designator, a net label, or a numbered wire to open a popup listing everywhere it appears; the arrow keys and `Enter` surf between the locations, `Esc` closes
- **View classes (layers)**: twelve display toggles — wires, symbols, reference designators, net labels, wire numbers, wire notes, harnesses, graphics, annotations, panel, frame, grid — on the ribbon View tab (display-only; outputs always contain everything, and wire notes are always exported because they are the drawing's primary information)
- **PLC I/O**: place a PLC module from the parts database (its `plc_module` definition gives the point count, the I/O direction and the maker's address scheme) and it appears as the parametric symbol `plc_di_{n}p` / `plc_do_{n}p`. The **I/O assignment table** (Reports tab → PLC I/O group) is one row per point: address, signal name and comment are yours to edit, while target and wire number are read out of the drawing. *Fill addresses* numbers a whole module from a start address in its own scheme — Mitsubishi octal (`X0…X7, X10`), Siemens byte.bit (`%I0.0`), Allen-Bradley word/bit (`I:0/0`). The table round-trips through CSV, so signal names can be typed in a spreadsheet. *Generate I/O sheet* builds a ladder page (a vertical `P24` bus with one rung per point, each labelled with its address and signal name) as **one undo step**; the generated page passes ERC with no findings. Editing the drawing afterwards — wiring a device onto a rung, numbering the wires — flows straight back into the target and wire-number columns and into the PLC I/O report
- **PLC I/O generation settings**: the ladder is either a vertical bus with horizontal rungs (default, JIS practice) or a **horizontal bus with vertical rungs** (the module lies under the bus with its points facing up, I/O running left to right; the rung labels alternate between two rows so neighbours do not overlap). **Module placement**: *new ladder per module* (default); *share if it fits* — the module goes onto the last generated I/O page (same style and rung spacing) when its points fit in the rungs left, one empty rung after the previous module, else onto a new page; *share and split* — the rungs left are filled in whole **split units** (4 / 8 / 16 points, default 8 = a common group) and the rest continues on new pages, a module larger than a page being spread over as many pages as it needs. Each split piece is drawn as a split module symbol `plc_di_{n}p_s{start}` whose pins keep the module's own point numbers (so the assignment table and the I/O report still read every point), with `continued → /n` and `← from /n` references at the cut. Everything a generation adds — new pages, the rungs added to a shared page, the assignment rows — is one undo step. *Save these settings with the project* (on by default) stores them in the project (`.mdkproj` format 14) as a separate undoable edit, and the dialog starts from them next time
- **Properties panel**: edit the reference designator and part number/value of the selection, a symbol's function label and pin table, a connector's mate designator, a note's anchor / colour / weight, a net label's tag shape, and a wire's colour, gauge, length and part number (the part number resolved from the wire-part table is shown as a placeholder)

## Symbol editor

Draw your own symbols instead of hand-writing JSON. Ribbon: Admin tab → Library group → *Symbol editor*.

- **Start from a copy**: the editor opens blank with a "New from a copy of…" popover listing every bundled and user symbol. Picking one gives you a draft with its shapes, connection points and attribute slots, an id of `{source}_copy` and the name suffixed `(copy)`. Nothing is written to disk until you save
- **Canvas**: a 2.5 mm grid with snap on. Line, rectangle, circle and arc are each drawn with one drag; text places the string from the coordinate bar; the attribute tool places the next unplaced slot; the connection-point tool drops a pin where you click and infers its direction from the symbol's outline. Click to select, drag to move, `Delete` to remove. Vertex editing, rotation and multi-select are not in this version
- **Attributes**: the four slots the model has — `TAG` (reference, required), `PART`, `DESC`, `RATING`. The value you type is only the sample drawn on the editor canvas; it is not stored in the symbol file
- **Connection points**: a table of number / direction / name, plus *+ Up / + Down / + Left / + Right* buttons. Points must sit on a grid intersection. The same number on two separate points is legitimate (a feed-through terminal), a duplicate at the same point is not
- **Rotation variants**: 0° / 90° / 180° / 270° previews drawn with the same renderer as the drawing. Variants are derived, never stored — point numbers and attribute positions follow the rotation automatically
- **Pre-save checks**: four fixed rows (grid, duplicate numbers, required slot, optional slots) with anything else listed after them. Unplaced optional attributes are a warning you can save through; the final verdict is Rust's, so what the panel accepts the save accepts
- **Saving**: `~/MadakeCAD/symbols/{id}.json` — the `SymbolDef` JSON as-is, always filed under the **User** category. An id that collides with a bundled or parametric symbol is refused, so a file dropped in later can never silently change how an existing drawing looks. If the save is refused nothing is written and every reason is reported. Saved symbols appear in the **User** category of the insert dialog immediately
- **Library follow-through**: drawings store only the symbol id, so re-saving a user symbol redraws the parts already placed with it. Deleting a symbol that is in use is refused unless you force it, and the refusal tells you how many instances there are
- Also reachable outside the UI: IPC, `GET /api/v1/symbols/user`, `POST /api/v1/symbols/user/save|delete`, `POST /api/v1/symbols/duplicate`, and the MCP tools `duplicate_symbol` / `save_user_symbol` / `delete_user_symbol` — so the AI assistant can build symbols too

## 2D panel layout

A second kind of sheet for the inside of the enclosure. The sheet tab row's **+** asks which kind you want; a panel sheet switches the ribbon to the Panel tab and the left panel to *Unplaced parts* on its own.

- **Panel entities**: enclosure (outline plus the mounting plate, inset 10 mm, moved as one), DIN rail (horizontal; the mounting line you press on, drawn as a 35 mm TS35 band), wiring duct (45° hatch anchored to the paper, so neighbouring ducts line up), footprint (the rectangle a part occupies) and the legend table. They are ordinary entities, so `⌘Z`, move and delete work exactly as on a schematic
- **Unplaced parts**: every designator on the schematic that has no footprint on any panel sheet — a relay's coil and contacts count as one device. Each row shows the designator, the part number, the outline size and where the size came from (*from parts DB* / *default* / *unregistered*). Click a row, then click the panel: the ghost is held by the part's centre, `R` turns it 90°, and the centre snaps onto a DIN rail's mounting line when you are within the band. Moving a placed part snaps the same way, and one `⌘Z` puts it back
- **Balloons (item numbers)**: *Balloon numbers* assigns 1…N to the footprints of the sheet in reading order — rows are split by the part's **centre** height, then left to right — and draws a balloon at each. The BOM's first column is that item number, so the layout table and the BOM always agree. The inverse command restores the numbers you had, so renumbering is one undo step
- **Legend table**: an entity that stores only its position; the rows are derived from the sheet's footprints every time it is drawn, so it can never fall out of date
- **Panel checks** (run from *Clearance check*, reported in the verification panel): `panel.overlap` — two footprints overlapping in area, edges merely touching is the normal look of a filled rail, so it is not reported; `panel.off_rail` — a part on no rail at all, not reported on a panel with no rails (parts screwed to the plate); `panel.rail_fill` — the fill ratio of every rail (sum of part widths ÷ rail length), a warning above 80 %; `panel.orphan_footprint` — a footprint whose designator is not on the schematic
- **Layout table report**: item no. / designator / part no. / X / Y / rail, as CSV, as a framed drawing sheet or inside the combined PDF. X and Y are the top-left corner of the drawn outline, and rails are numbered from the top of the panel
- **Nameplates**: Panel tab → *Nameplate*, then click a footprint to attach one (it sits centred below or above the part and moves with it) or click empty space for a free-standing plate. A plate has up to two lines, a text height, a size and a part number; *follow the schematic description* keeps line 1 equal to the function label of the symbol with the same designator. Nameplate part numbers add one quantity row each to the BOM
- **Dimensions**: Panel tab → *Dimension*, then click two points. Clicking on a footprint ties that end to the part's centre, so the value follows when the part moves; the direction (horizontal/vertical) is the larger of the two distances. The properties panel shows the current value read-only
- **Align / distribute**: select two or more footprints (Shift+click; the first one you pick is the reference) and open *Align*: left / centre / right / top / middle / bottom, or distribute horizontally / vertically — evenly between the outer two, or at a fixed centre pitch. Each is one command (one `⌘Z`). Parts on a DIN rail are never moved up or down off their rail
- **In-panel wiring diagram**: Panel tab → *Panel wiring* (or `madake export panel-wiring`) lists, for every footprint, the schematic wires on each of its pins — terminal / wire no. / destination (`designator:pin` or net label) / colour / sq — as CSV or as framed drawing pages. It is derived from the schematic each time; fix wire numbers there
- Panel sheets are outside ERC and the electrical checks (they carry no wiring)
